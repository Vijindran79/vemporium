Dispatch outbox worker

Closes the fulfilment loop: a paid order whose WhatsApp failed is a customer who
paid for something nobody is making. Module 2 improves conversion; this stops
losing orders, so it went first.

- GET /api/cron/process-outbox on a 5-minute Vercel cron, plus vercel.json
- the claim is a conditional updateMany on status = PENDING. Without the status
  guard in the where, two workers send the same supplier message
- PROCESSING rows carry lockedAt and workerId, and anything older than 5 minutes
  is reclaimed. A worker that crashes mid-flight cannot release its own lock, so
  without this one crash strands paid orders in PROCESSING forever — the most
  common way a job queue silently dies
- backoff doubles from 1 minute, capped at 1 hour. Uncapped doubling is
  arithmetically correct and commercially useless: an order alert scheduled for
  3am reaches a karigah who has stopped looking at WhatsApp. The whole 5-attempt
  budget spans ~15 minutes, so it is worth capping at all
- FAILED is deliberately not a resting state. Rows end SENT or DEAD, so "dead"
  and "retryable" are never confused, and DEAD counts are logged loudly
- a POST /reclaim variant that recovers stranded rows WITHOUT sending, so an
  operator can inspect after a crash without also re-sending everything

drainOutbox moved out of fulfilment.ts into lib/outbox.ts so the immediate
post-commit drain and the retry worker share one recordOutcome(). Two
implementations of "what happens after a send attempt" will drift, and drift here
means an order that silently never reaches its workshop.

The cron endpoint requires Authorization: Bearer $CRON_SECRET, constant-time
compare, and fails CLOSED in production when the secret is missing — unlike the
legacy internal webhook which degrades open in dev. An unprotected worker lets
anyone with the URL send WhatsApp from our Twilio account to our suppliers, and
can drive the queue into a state where real orders never send.

A test caught a real bug while writing this: retryDelayMs(NaN) returned NaN,
because Math.max(0, NaN) is NaN and Math.min does not catch it. That becomes an
Invalid Date in nextAttemptAt, which Prisma rejects, taking down the whole
worker pass. Fixed by guarding the input rather than the result.

Verified live: no auth header -> 401, wrong secret -> 401, correct secret -> 500
from the unreachable database (not 401), with PrismaClientInitializationError and
no code errors in the log.
